# Outreach hooks: towing (Subchapter M)

One of these goes in each first email: a question a towing crew actually asks, a two- or
three-sentence answer, and the exact citation. Every answer was checked against the regulation
text in the RegKnots corpus on 2026-09-27. Do not edit an answer without re-checking the
section; do not add a hook without doing the same. The outreach task picks the best fit for the
company and never writes a new regulatory answer itself.

## H1 — Safety orientation for a new deckhand
**Q:** Does a new deckhand need a safety orientation before the boat gets underway?
**A:** Yes. Before getting underway for the first time on a particular towing vessel, every
crewmember needs a safety orientation covering ten topics, from emergency duties and lifesaving
gear to fall-overboard prevention, PPE, egress and the tow. It must be logged with the date,
the topics, and the names and signatures of who gave and received it (46 CFR 140.410). Someone
already oriented on a sister boat only needs what is different on this one (140.410(c)).
**Fits:** any towing company; strongest for fleets with crew rotation.

## H2 — Drills and instruction
**Q:** How often does a towing crew have to do emergency drills and instruction under Subchapter M?
**A:** Each crewmember must get the required instruction every year: fire response, launching the
skiff if it is emergency gear, and davit-launched liferafts or a rescue boat if installed. Drills
are run on board where practicable, with the whole crew, as if the emergency were real
(46 CFR 140.420). Officers holding an endorsement are exempt from some of the instruction items
(140.420(d)(5)).
**Fits:** any towing company.

## H3 — Navigation assessment
**Q:** What has to go into the navigation assessment before a towboat gets underway?
**A:** The officer in charge of the watch must complete one before getting underway, covering at
least 13 items, including currents, water depth and river stage, weather and visibility, traffic,
pilothouse equipment status, air draft, horizontal clearance, locks, hazards, notices to mariners,
the tow's configuration and the watch's experience. It has to be kept current, and every oncoming
watch reviews it (46 CFR 140.635).
**Fits:** inland and river operators (locks, river stage, bridge transits).

## H4 — What a Subchapter M boat must record
**Q:** What records does a Subchapter M towboat have to keep?
**A:** Ten items go in the Towing Vessel Record, the official logbook or your TSMS: personnel
records, safety orientations, drills and instruction, examinations and tests, navigation safety
equipment, navigation assessments, navigation safety training, oil residue discharges, towing
gear inspections, and fire detection and fixed extinguishing records (46 CFR 140.915). Electronic
entries need the date, the time and the name of the person, and a correction must preserve the
original entry.
**Fits:** any towing company; strongest with a TSMS or an upcoming TPO audit.

## H5 — Fire extinguisher checks
**Q:** Who can do the monthly fire extinguisher checks on a towboat?
**A:** The owner, operator, person in charge or a designated crewmember can do the monthly NFPA 10
inspections. Annual maintenance of rechargeable extinguishers needs certified servicing personnel
(a state- or locally licensed servicing agency counts); non-rechargeable ones can be maintained by
the crew. All portable extinguishers and fixed systems must be inspected or tested at least every
12 months (46 CFR 142.240(a)).
**Fits:** small operators without a shore-side safety department.

## H6 — Towing gear records
**Q:** What towing gear records does a TPO auditor or the Coast Guard expect to see?
**A:** A record of the type, size and service of each towline, face wire and spring line used to
make the boat fast to its tow, with the dates of each examination, which gear was examined and who
examined it. The results of the 33 CFR 164.76 inspections go in the Towing Vessel Record, the
logbook or your TSMS (46 CFR 140.820).
**Fits:** harbor tug and fleeting operators; anyone with face wires.

# Schools (niche `school`, offer `practice`) — 2026-10-02

Schools are offered the free practice page for their students, not a subscription. These
are product facts, not regulatory answers; do not add regulatory claims to a school email.

## S1 — Free practice questions for their students
**Point:** RegKnot's free practice page uses the NMC's own published sample exams: deck
(Rules of the Road, deck general, navigation, stability), engine (motor, steam, electrical,
QMED) and endorsements (lifeboatman, tankship, barge). Students pick a topic and answer 10
questions; each answer shows the correct choice and, where one applies, the regulation it
comes from. No sign-up, no ads, free for anyone. Instructors are welcome to send their classes.
**Fits:** any school with license, endorsement or STCW courses. Mention the courses the row
lists (`service`) in the first sentence. The `practice` offer line the script adds already
describes the page, so the body needs only that first sentence and one more from this point
(the topics that match their courses, or that instructors are welcome to send their classes).

# Subchapter M TPOs (niche `tpo`, offer `partner`) — 2026-10-02

The six Coast Guard-approved third-party organizations audit TSMSs and survey towing vessels
(46 CFR 139.115). The email asks for a short conversation, not a sale. One per run at most;
Blake may rewrite these before sending.

## P1 — The records their auditors check
**Point:** Operators keep ten kinds of records in the Towing Vessel Record, the official
logbook or the TSMS (46 CFR 140.915), and crews ask what goes where. RegKnot answers those
questions with the exact citation, set up for each boat. Ask whether a short call makes sense
about whether it would help the operators they audit.
**Fits:** all six TPOs.
