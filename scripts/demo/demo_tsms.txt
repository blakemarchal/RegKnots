GULF COAST MARINE TOWING (DEMONSTRATION COMPANY)
TOWING SAFETY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM: EXCERPTS
This is a fictional document for RegKnot demonstrations. The company, vessel and people are invented.

1. Purpose and scope
This Towing Safety Management System (TSMS) sets out how Gulf Coast Marine Towing operates the M/V Bay Pioneer safely and in line with Subchapter M (46 CFR Parts 136 to 144). It applies to every crewmember, to shoreside management, and to anyone working aboard on the company's behalf. Where this TSMS and a regulation differ, the regulation governs.

2. Responsibilities
The Master has overriding authority for the safety of the vessel, crew and tow, and may stop any operation he or she considers unsafe. The Mate (Pilot) stands the second watch and acts for the Master when the Master is off watch. Deckhands report every hazard, injury and near miss to the officer on watch. The Designated Person Ashore is the Operations Manager, reachable at all hours through the dispatch line. Any crewmember may call a stop-work at any time without penalty.

3. Watches and rest
The Master and the Mate (Pilot) stand six-hour watches. Company policy: no crewmember works more than 12 hours in any 24-hour period except in an emergency or drill. A crewmember who feels too fatigued to work safely tells the Master, who adjusts the watch. Crew change is every 14 days at the Port Arthur dock.

4. Pre-departure checks
Before getting underway the officer on watch completes the pre-departure checklist: steering tested from each station, engine and generator alarms tested, navigation lights and the radar checked, VHF radios checked on channels 13 and 16, and the towline, bitts and winch inspected. The checklist is signed and kept in the vessel log for 12 months.

5. Towline and towing gear
The deck crew inspect the towline and towing gear every 30 days and after any heavy strain. A towline with broken strands, kinks, heat damage or more than 10 percent wear at any point is taken out of service and tagged. Each inspection, renewal and end-for-end is recorded in the towline log with the date and the inspector's initials.

6. Drills and training
Company policy: fire, abandon-vessel and man-overboard drills are held at least once a month, rotated so every crewmember takes part. New crewmembers receive a vessel orientation before their first watch, covering the location of lifesaving and firefighting equipment, emergency stations and alarm signals. Drills and orientations are recorded in the vessel log.

7. Reporting incidents
Any injury, fire, grounding, allision, collision, pollution or loss of steering or propulsion is reported at once to the Master and to the Designated Person Ashore. The Designated Person Ashore notifies the nearest Coast Guard Sector when a marine casualty occurs and files the written Report of Marine Casualty (Form CG-2692) as the regulations require. Near misses are reported on the company near-miss form within 24 hours.

8. Fuel and oil transfers
Fuel transfers follow the posted transfer procedures. Before a transfer the person in charge holds a pre-transfer conference with the facility, confirms the scuppers are plugged and the containment is ready, and keeps the transfer under continuous watch. Every transfer is recorded in the Oil Record Book.

9. Internal audits and management review
The Operations Manager audits this TSMS on board and ashore at least once a year. Findings are logged with a corrective action and a due date. External audits by the company's Third-Party Organization follow 46 CFR Part 138. Management reviews the audit results, incidents and near misses every quarter and updates this TSMS when needed.
